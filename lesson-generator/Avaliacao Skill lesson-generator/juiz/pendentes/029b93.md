# Adiabatic flame temperature of methane

[problem] 

Methane (CH4) is burned completely with 20% excess dry air in an adiabatic combustor. The air is 21% O2 and 79% N2 by mole. Fuel and air enter at T0 = 298.15 K. The heat of combustion at 298.15 K, with water as vapor, is ΔHc = −802.6 kJ per mol of CH4. The ideal-gas heat capacities follow Cp/R = A + B·T + D/T², with T in K and R = 8.314 J/(mol·K). The coefficients are: CO2, A = 5.457, B = 1.045×10⁻³, D = −1.157×10⁵; H2O, A = 3.470, B = 1.450×10⁻³, D = 0.121×10⁵; N2, A = 3.280, B = 0.593×10⁻³, D = 0.040×10⁵; O2, A = 3.639, B = 0.506×10⁻³, D = −0.227×10⁵.

What is the adiabatic flame temperature, in K, with 20% excess air?

What is the adiabatic flame temperature, in K, with 100% excess air?

What percentage of excess air gives an adiabatic flame temperature of 1500 K?

The heat released by the combustion warms the products, and, because no heat leaves the combustor, the products end up as hot as the chemistry allows. This is the adiabatic flame temperature. At the end, you will have a program that solves the energy balance for any excess of air and finds the excess that gives a chosen temperature.

## Model

### Mass balance of component CH4, in moles

 ⟦accumulation of CH4 = in − out + generation⟧ 

(principle) CH4 does not appear or disappear for no reason. The control volume is the combustor; at steady state the accumulation is zero.

 ⟦0 = n_CH 4 , in {in} − n_CH 4 , out {out} + ν_CH 4 ⋅ ξ {generation}⟧ 

nCH4,in, nCH4,out — moles of CH4 entering and leaving the combustor, mol

νCH4 — stoichiometric coefficient of CH4: −1, because it is consumed

ξ — extent of reaction, mol of reaction per mol of CH4 fed

(translation) Each word becomes a molar flow. The reaction is CH4 + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O, so the coefficients are −1, −2, +1 and +2 for CH4, O2, CO2 and H2O.

 ⟦n_CH 4 , out = 0⟧ 

(assumption) Complete combustion: no CH4 leaves. If some CH4 escaped, the outlet would contain it and the extent of reaction would be smaller than 1.

 ⟦n_CH 4 , in = 1 mol⟧ 

(definition) Basis of the calculation: 1 mol of CH4 fed. All the amounts in this model are per mol of CH4.

[final] 

 ⟦ξ = 1 mol of reaction per mol of CH4⟧ 

(algebra) Substituting the assumption and the basis into the balance: 0 = 1 − 0 − ξ.

Each mole of CH4 makes one reaction, so all the carbon ends up as CO2 and all the hydrogen as H2O. The other balances use this extent.

### Mass balance of component O2, in moles

 ⟦accumulation of O2 = in − out + generation⟧ 

(principle) O2 comes with the air and is consumed by the reaction. The control volume is the combustor, at steady state.

 ⟦0 = n_O 2 , in {in} − n_O 2 , out {out} + ν_O 2 ⋅ ξ {generation}⟧ 

nO2,in, nO2,out — moles of O2 entering with the air and leaving in the products, mol per mol of CH4

νO2 — stoichiometric coefficient of O2: −2

(translation) The same form as for CH4, with the coefficient of O2.

 ⟦n_O 2 , in = 2 ( 1 + e )⟧ 

e — excess of air as a fraction: 0.20 for 20%, 1.00 for 100%

(definition) The reaction needs 2 mol of O2 per mol of CH4; the excess supplies the same amount, multiplied by e.

[final] 

 ⟦n_O 2 , out = 2 e⟧ 

(algebra) Substituting ξ = 1 and the definition above: 0 = 2(1 + e) − nO2,out − 2.

The excess oxygen leaves unburned. At 20% excess it is 2 × 0.20 = 0.40 mol per mol of CH4.

### Mass balance of component N2, in moles

 ⟦accumulation of N2 = in − out + generation⟧ 

(principle) N2 is carried by the air and does not react, so it leaves in the products.

 ⟦0 = n_N 2 , in − n_N 2 , out⟧ 

(translation) With no generation, the balance is in minus out.

[final] 

 ⟦n_N 2 , out = (0.79)/(0.21) ⋅ 2 ( 1 + e )⟧ 

(definition) The air has 79 mol of N2 for every 21 mol of O2, so the N2 is the O2 entering multiplied by 0.79/0.21.

### Mass balance of component CO2, in moles

 ⟦accumulation of CO2 = in − out + generation⟧ 

(principle) CO2 is not in the feed; it is formed by the reaction and leaves in the products.

 ⟦0 = 0 − n_CO 2 , out + 1 ⋅ ξ⟧ 

(translation) The coefficient of CO2 is +1, so the generation is the extent.

[final] 

 ⟦n_CO 2 , out = 1⟧ 

(algebra) With ξ = 1, one mol of CO2 leaves per mol of CH4.

### Mass balance of component H2O, in moles

 ⟦accumulation of H2O = in − out + generation⟧ 

(principle) H2O is not in the feed; it is formed by the reaction and leaves in the products.

 ⟦0 = 0 − n_H 2 O , out + 2 ⋅ ξ⟧ 

(translation) The coefficient of H2O is +2, so the generation is twice the extent.

[final] 

 ⟦n_H 2 O , out = 2⟧ 

(algebra) With ξ = 1, two mol of H2O leave per mol of CH4.

### Overall energy balance of the combustor, in joules

 ⟦accumulation of energy = energy in − energy out + heat in − work out⟧ 

(principle) Energy is conserved. The control volume is the combustor; at steady state the accumulation is zero.

 ⟦0 = H_in {energy in} − H_out {energy out} + Q {heat in} − W {work out}⟧ 

Hin, Hout — enthalpy of the feed at 298.15 K and of the products at T, J

Q, W — heat entering and shaft work leaving the combustor, J

(translation) Each term is an energy flow carried by the streams, plus heat and work at the walls and shaft.

 ⟦Q = 0 , W = 0⟧ 

(assumption) The combustor is adiabatic and has no shaft work, and the kinetic and potential energies of the streams are negligible. If heat were lost through the walls, the flame would be colder than computed here.

 ⟦H_out − H_in = ΔH_c + ∑ {i} n_i , out ⋅ ∫ C_p , i d T {heating the products}⟧ 

(definition) Path: the reactants burn at 298.15 K, releasing ΔHc, and then the products are heated from 298.15 K to T. The reactants are already at 298.15 K, so this path gives the same change of enthalpy.

Here ΔHc is the heat of combustion, −802.6 kJ per mol of CH4, and the sum is over the products.

 ⟦h_i ( T ) = ∫ C_p , i d T⟧ 

hi — heat to warm 1 mol of species i from 298.15 K to T, J/mol; the integral is evaluated with the correlation given below

(definition) The sensible enthalpy of each species, which the next step evaluates.

[final] 

 ⟦∑ {i} n_i , out ⋅ h_i ( T ) {heat that warms the products} = − ΔH_c {heat released}⟧ 

(algebra) With Q = W = 0 and the path above, the heat that warms the products equals the heat released.

The products take up, as sensible heat, all the energy the combustion releases. Because each species' heat grows with T at a different rate, the value of T that balances the equation is the adiabatic flame temperature. It is the only unknown.

[eq-block] Heat capacity of a gas: 

 ⟦C_p / R = A + B ⋅ T + (D)/(T^(2) )⟧ 

A, B, D — coefficients of each gas, in the table below

R — gas constant, 8.314 J/(mol·K)

T — temperature of the gas, K

The correlation is given. The integral of Cp from 298.15 K to T is R times [A(T − T0) + B/2 (T² − T0²) − D(1/T − 1/T0)], so hi is that expression with the coefficients of species i.

[technique] 

[technique-name] Root finding, linear solve

The flame temperature is the value of T that makes the energy balance true; T enters as a power and as 1/T, so there is no simple formula. To solve this kind of problem, we will use root finding: the program tries temperatures in an interval where the balance changes sign and narrows it until the balance is zero. The excess question is linear in the excess, so a direct formula solves it, and root finding checks it. In Python, the function that does root finding is brentq, from the library scipy.optimize. See the next section to learn how to use it.

### What the products contain

[purpose] A function that gives the moles of each product for any excess of air, and the energy residual that is zero at the flame temperature.

```
def products(excess):
 """Moles of each product per mol of CH4, for an excess of air
 (0.20 means 20%)."""
 o2_fed = 2 * (1 + excess) # 2 mol O2 to burn, plus excess
 return {
 "CO2": 1.0,
 "H2O": 2.0,
 "O2": o2_fed - 2, # the excess O2 left over
 "N2": o2_fed * 0.79 / 0.21, # the N2 that came with the air
 }

def energy_residual(T, excess):
 """Heat to warm the products from T0 to T, minus the heat
 released. It is zero at the adiabatic flame temperature."""
 n = products(excess)
 heat = sum(n[g] * h_sensible(g, T) for g in n)
```

[how] brentq(f, a, b, args=(…)) finds the root of f between a and b. Here: f is energy_residual, which brentq calls many times with different temperatures in T; a = T0 and b = 4000 K are the interval, a bracket, in which the residual must change sign (it is negative at 298.15 K, where no heat is needed, and positive at 4000 K); args=(excess,) is the path by which the excess reaches each call, as the second argument of energy_residual. The comma makes it a tuple of one element. brentq stops when the interval is narrower than its tolerance, 2×10⁻¹² K by default, far below the 0.1 K printed: the tolerance is the precision criterion of the root. It returns the temperature. flame_temperature packs that call into one name.
