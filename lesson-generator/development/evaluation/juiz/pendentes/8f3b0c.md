# Compressibility of carbon dioxide from the Peng-Robinson equation

[problem] 

Carbon dioxide is stored at T = 320 K and P = 80 bar. Using the Peng-Robinson equation of state, with the critical temperature Tc = 304.13 K, the critical pressure Pc = 73.77 bar, the acentric factor ω = 0.224, the molar mass M = 44.01 g/mol and the gas constant R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), compute the following.

The compressibility factor Z, which is the ratio of the real molar volume to the ideal gas molar volume, dimensionless.

The molar volume of the gas, in cm³/mol.

The density of the gas, in kg/m³.

The molar volume that the ideal gas law gives at the same conditions, in cm³/mol.

At 80 bar, carbon dioxide is far from an ideal gas, so the ideal gas law gives a wrong volume. The Peng-Robinson equation corrects it, but to use it you must solve for the volume, which leads to a cubic equation. At the end, you will have a program that gives Z, the molar volume and the density, next to the ideal gas value.

## Model

There is no balance in this problem. The model is an equation of state, which relates the pressure, the molar volume and the temperature of the gas.

[eq-block] Peng-Robinson equation of state: 

 ⟦P = (R T)/(V − b) − (a)/(V^(2) + 2 b V − b^(2) )⟧ 

P — pressure of the gas, Pa

V — molar volume of the gas, the volume of one mole, m³/mol

T — temperature of the gas, K

a — attraction between the molecules, Pa·m⁶/mol²

b — volume occupied by the molecules themselves, m³/mol

The first term is the ideal gas law, RT/V, with the volume reduced by b, because the molecules take up space. The second term lowers the pressure, because the molecules attract each other. With a = 0 and b = 0, the equation becomes the ideal gas law.

[eq-block] Parameter a: 

 ⟦a = 0.45724 (R^(2) T_c^(2) )/(P_c ) α⟧ 

Tc, Pc — critical temperature and pressure, K and Pa

α — correction for the temperature, dimensionless; it equals 1 at Tc

Each substance has its own a, computed from its critical point. It depends on the temperature only through α.

[eq-block] Alpha function: 

 ⟦α = [ 1 + κ ( 1 − √((T)/(T_c )) ) ]^(2)⟧ 

κ — a factor that depends only on the substance, dimensionless

The square root of T/Tc measures how far the gas is from its critical temperature.

[eq-block] Kappa, from the acentric factor: 

 ⟦κ = 0.37464 + 1.54226 ω − 0.26992 ω^(2)⟧ 

ω — acentric factor, given in the problem, dimensionless

An empirical fit that turns the acentric factor into the number κ; it is the same for every temperature.

[eq-block] Parameter b: 

 ⟦b = 0.07780 (R T_c )/(P_c )⟧ 

Computed from the critical point only: it does not depend on the temperature.

[eq-block] Cubic form in Z: 

 ⟦Z^(3) − ( 1 − B ) Z^(2) + ( A − 3 B^(2) − 2 B ) Z − ( A B − B^(2) − B^(3) ) = 0⟧ 

 ⟦Z = (P V)/(R T) , A = (a P)/(( R T )^(2) ) , B = (b P)/(R T)⟧ 

Z — compressibility factor, PV/(RT), dimensionless; it is 1 for an ideal gas

A, B — dimensionless groups of the pressure, the temperature and the parameters

Writing V as ZRT/P in the equation of state and multiplying through removes the volume. What is left is a cubic in Z, whose coefficients depend only on A and B, which are known once P and T are.

Parameter | Value | Unit | Source | 

R | 8.314 | J/(mol·K) | given | 

T | 320 | K | given | 

P | 80 | bar | given in the program, converted to Pa: 80 × 10⁵ Pa | 

Tc | 304.13 | K | given | 

Pc | 73.77 | bar | given in the program, converted to Pa: 73.77 × 10⁵ Pa | 

ω | 0.224 | — | given | 

M | 44.01 | g/mol | given | 

κ | 0.7066 | — | derived from ω, in the eq. of κ | 

α | 0.9639 | — | derived at 320 K, in the eq. of α | 

a | 0.3820 | Pa·m⁶/mol² | derived from α, in the eq. of a | 

b | 26.67 | cm³/mol | derived from the critical point, in the eq. of b | 

[technique] 

[technique-name] Polynomial roots

The problem is to find the value of Z that makes a cubic polynomial equal to zero, that is, a polynomial root. To solve this kind of problem, we will use polynomial root finding: the roots are computed directly from the coefficients, with no initial guess and no trial and error. It fits because a cubic has up to three roots, and only the real one has a physical meaning, so the program has to select it. In Python, the function that does this is roots, from the library NumPy. See the next section to learn how to use it.

### Compressibility factor as a function of pressure

[purpose] Write, once, the procedure that gives Z for any pressure: it builds the cubic, finds its roots and keeps the physical one.

```
def Z_of_P(P):
 """Compressibility factor at pressure P (Pa), at temperature T."""
 A = a * P / (R * T)**2 # dimensionless
 B = b * P / (R * T) # dimensionless
 c2 = -(1 - B) # coefficient of Z**2
 c1 = A - 3*B**2 - 2*B # coefficient of Z
 c0 = -(A*B - B**2 - B**3) # constant term
 roots = np.roots([1, c2, c1, c0]) # the three roots of the cubic
 real = roots[np.isreal(roots)].real # keep the real roots
 return real.max() # the largest real root
```

[how] 

def Z_of_P(P):: defines a function named Z_of_P. P is its argument: the value that the caller gives when it calls the function, here a pressure in Pa.

"""...""": a docstring, a note that describes the function. Python does not run it.

np.roots([1, c2, c1, c0]): receives the coefficients of a polynomial, from the highest power down, so [1, c2, c1, c0] means Z³ + c2·Z² + c1·Z + c0. It returns the three roots, as a list of numbers that may be complex.

np.isreal(roots): returns a list of True or False, one per root: True when the root has no imaginary part.

roots[...]: selects from the list the items where the mask is True.

.real: an attribute of each number; it gives its real part.

.max(): a method, returns the largest value of the list. return sends that value back to the caller.

The function uses a, b, R and T without defining them: Python takes them from the program above.
