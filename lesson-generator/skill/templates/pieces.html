Complete derivation

Mass balance of component A, in moles

  1. accumulation of A=in−out−consumption

    principleA does not appear or disappear for no reason. The control volume is the liquid inside the reactor.

  2. d(V⋅CA)dtaccumulation = F⋅CA0in − F⋅CAout − rA⋅Vconsumption
    • CA — concentration of A in the reactor and at the outlet, mol/L
    • V — volume of liquid in the reactor, L

    translationEach word becomes a molar flow of A.

  3. rA=k⋅CA

    kineticsFirst order, as the problem states.

  4. V=constant

    assumptionThe same flow goes in and out. If the tank were filling, the volume would need a balance of its own.

  5. dCAdtaccumulation of A = 1τ(CA0−CA)in minus out − k⋅CAconsumption

    algebradefinitionEverything divided by V, with τ = V/F.

    The stream pulls the tank toward the feed concentration, and the reaction consumes A; the steady state is where the two cancel out.

Complementary equations

Power law

rA=k⋅CAn
  • k — rate constant, in a unit that depends on the order: (mol/L)1−n/min
  • n — reaction order with respect to A, dimensionless

The rate grows with the concentration raised to the order: with n = 2, doubling CA quadruples the rate.

Arrhenius

k=k0⋅exp(−EaR⋅T)
  • k0 — pre-exponential factor, in the unit of k
  • Ea — activation energy, J/mol
  • R — gas constant, 8.314 J/(mol·K)

Use it when the problem gives k₀ and Eₐ. Eₐ and R in the same energy unit, T in kelvin.

Arrhenius with a reference temperature

k(T)=kref⋅exp[EaR(1Tref−1T)]
  • kref — rate constant measured at Tref
  • Tref — temperature at which kref was measured, K

Use it when the problem gives k at one temperature, instead of k₀: at T = Tref the bracket vanishes and k = kref.
